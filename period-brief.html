The period in five findings

Finding 1 · assessed with high confidence

The June escalation episode

Across the 45-day window the corpus carried 26,464 sourced events, with every one of the 45 days sourced (coverage 1.0 against a 0.75 floor). Daily volume ran near a baseline median of roughly 500–640 events until 7 June, when it reached 1,052 (z 7.69) — the sharpest single-day anomaly of the window — followed by the window's highest count, 1,231, on 8 June (z 5.09). 9 June fell back to 816 (z 1.55, below the bar), and 10 June rose again to 1,048 (z 2.79), closing the episode. No flagged day was rejected by the source-composition control — the surge was not an artifact of a change in who was reporting. escalation_detection · 2026-08-31 · sweep_004 · Tier A

Finding 2 · assessed with moderate confidence; false-discovery share stated

Narrative realignment at the episode boundary

Of 1,515 eligible narratives, 261 shifted at the boundary between the window's two halves — 8 June, the heart of the escalation episode. 130 cleared the admission cut fixed before the run: per-shift confidence at or above 0.60 plus corroboration by an independent method. This pass carries a false-discovery share of 0.29 — roughly 29 of every 100 detections are expected to be false — stated here and wherever this method is cited. The admitted shifts clustered around ceasefire and UN Security Council resolution framing, claims of United States participation in strikes, Iran's negotiation stance, and Strait of Hormuz closure threats. Shift dating carries window-boundary resolution — “at the boundary”, never a specific day — and corroboration by date says something changed then; it does not verify the framing content of any single shift. narrative_shift_detection · 2026-08-31 · sweep_002 · conf 0.6085 / FDR 0.29 · Tier A

Finding 3 · assessed with moderate confidence; the map speaks for a third of the window

Concentrated geography, honestly bounded

Of the window's 26,464 events, 9,156 — 34.6% — carried plottable coordinates; every map on this page speaks for those and no more. How this was made

The corpus behind this page more than doubled between the original calibration pass and this one — 11,189 to 27,461 events, 16,067 to 41,390 claims — with the backfill closed on 31 August 2026. Every capability re-ran on the same 45-day window under its original pre-registered decision rule; the thresholds were re-adopted and frozen, in writing, before any result was computed, and the original-versus-expanded delta table above is published unedited, including the one flagged-day revision and the fall in plottable share (50.7% to 34.6%). One amendment to the frozen rules was made mid-run (an actor-eligibility exclusion), approved and disclosed with its date and reason — the change log is part of the record, and an empty change log was the goal.

A narrative shift appears on this page only if it cleared a confidence cut of 0.60, fixed before the run, and was corroborated by a method that reads neither the narrative layer nor claim embeddings; the pass's false-discovery share (0.29) travels with every citation of it.

The prose readouts were drafted by the platform's copilot and then audited against mechanical re-derivation from the stored result envelopes: 21 claims were superseded and 8 corrected — among them one invented detail (a query cap that was never hit) and two misplaced severity dates — and the superseded text is preserved, struck through, in the published run output. All figures on this page were rendered mechanically from the saved result specifications; no language model drew or adjusted any picture.

Named events, dates and actors on this page passed a founder verification pass against the corpus. A flagged day is a day whose reporting volume behaved like escalation on this corpus — a change in who is reporting is not a change in the world, and the composition control enforced that. Nothing on this page makes a forward-looking claim: the window is retrospective, so every sentence can be checked — or refused.
